# RankMonk blog: editorial guidelines

These rules are read by the daily publishing routine (and by any human writer). Edit this file to change how posts are written; the routine picks up changes on its next run.

## Who we write for

Owners and marketing managers of local businesses in India (clinics, restaurants, salons, gyms, retail stores, showrooms, real estate site offices, coaching centres, bank branches), multi-location brands, and the agencies and consultants who manage them. They are busy, practical and not SEO specialists. They want to know what to do this week.

## Topic map (rotate categories; never repeat a topic already published)

Categories (exact strings): `Local SEO`, `Google Business Profile`, `Reviews`, `AI search`, `Multi-location`.

Topic ideas, each aimed at a real search query:

- **Local SEO**: how to rank higher on Google Maps; "near me" searches explained; what a geo-grid shows that a single rank check hides; local citations and NAP consistency; local SEO for a new business; local landing pages for multi-city businesses; local SEO checklist by industry (dentists, restaurants, salons, gyms, real estate, coaching institutes, car dealers, hotels); tracking the right local SEO metrics; service-area businesses vs storefronts.
- **Google Business Profile**: complete setup and verification; choosing primary and secondary categories; writing the business description; photos and videos that help; posts and offers; products and services; Q&A; attributes; holiday and special hours; why profiles get suspended and how to reinstate; handling suggested edits; duplicate listings; moving address; managing many profiles.
- **Reviews**: how to get more Google reviews legitimately (no incentives, no gating); replying to negative reviews with examples; reply templates by industry; fake review removal requests; review velocity and recency; using review themes to fix operations.
- **AI search**: how ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews pick local businesses (only what is publicly documented or observable, and say so); making your business easy for AI to describe; structured data for local businesses; tracking AI mentions.
- **Multi-location**: consistency across branches; bulk hours updates for festivals (Diwali, Eid, Christmas, regional holidays); branch-level reporting; governance for franchise and dealer networks; store locator pages.

Seasonal hooks for India are good when timely (festival hours, admission season for education, wedding season for salons, monsoon for home services), but only if the advice is real.

## Keyword approach

- Pick one primary query per post (e.g. "how to get more google reviews"). Put it naturally in the title, the first 100 words, one H2, and the meta description.
- Add 3 to 6 closely related secondary phrases where they fit naturally, plus Indian variants when real ("near me", city examples like Indiranagar, Koramangala, Andheri, Baner, Gurgaon).
- Never stuff keywords. If a sentence reads worse because of a keyword, remove the keyword.

## Quality bar (no AI slop)

- **Accuracy first.** Only state facts you are confident are true and current. Google's local ranking factors are relevance, distance and prominence (Google's own help article). Do not invent statistics, studies, percentages, quotes, case studies, customer names or results. If you give a number, it must be a documented fact (e.g. a platform limit) or clearly framed as an example ("say a clinic has 40 reviews…").
- **Specific and practical.** Every section should tell the reader what to do: steps, examples, a checklist, a sample reply, a before/after. Use Indian examples (a dental clinic in Indiranagar, a biryani outlet in Hyderabad, a gym in HSR Layout).
- **Plain English**, short paragraphs (2–4 sentences), active voice, second person. Explain any jargon the first time.
- **Banned phrases**: "in today's fast-paced world", "in the digital age", "delve", "dive in", "game-changer", "unlock", "unleash", "elevate", "supercharge", "leverage" (as a verb), "robust", "seamless", "navigate the landscape", "it's important to note", "in conclusion", "look no further", "whether you're a … or a …", "the ever-evolving". No emoji. Avoid em dashes; use commas, colons or full stops.
- No filler intro. Open with the reader's problem or the answer in the first two sentences.
- Length: 1,300–2,200 words. Longer only if the topic needs it.

## Structure (Markdown)

- Do **not** include the title as a `#` heading (the page adds it). Start with the intro paragraph.
- 4–8 `##` sections with descriptive headings (questions people search work well). `###` for sub-steps.
- At least one list or numbered steps; a table when comparing options.
- A short `## Frequently asked questions` section with 3–4 `###` questions near the end is encouraged.
- End with a brief, useful wrap-up paragraph (not titled "Conclusion") and, if natural, one line inviting readers to see their own rankings with a link to /contact.
- Links: internal as relative paths (`/features/reviews`). External links only to authoritative sources (Google Business Profile Help, Google Search Central) and only when certain the URL exists.

## Metadata rules

- `title`: 45–65 characters ideally (10–120 allowed), specific, includes the primary query, no clickbait, no year unless the content is year-specific.
- `slug`: short, lowercase, hyphenated, primary query (e.g. `how-to-get-more-google-reviews`).
- `description`: 140–160 characters, a plain promise of what the reader gets. Used as the meta description.
- `keywords`: the primary query plus 3–6 secondary phrases.
- `tags`: 2–5 short topic tags.
- `cover_image_url` + `cover_image_alt`: choose one from the catalogue below that fits; the alt text must describe the photo.
